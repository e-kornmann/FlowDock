## Background

Before software I worked for years as a graphic designer. Even then I kept looking for ways to optimise and automate my work, starting with small scripts that took repetitive tasks off my hands. That grew into an intensive full-stack bootcamp and a switch to software development that I haven't regretted for a moment.

My design background still shapes how I build software: I think from the user first. If the UX isn't right, what is the point of the feature?

---

## What drives me

I'm fascinated by the balance between simplicity and chaos, in code, nature, music and art.

What I enjoy most about software development is taking a complex problem and bringing it back to something logical, clear and maintainable.

---

## Where I'm heading

**AI in software development.** At first I was wary of AI. I was afraid it would take away the creative puzzling, the part of the job I enjoy most. I've since found that you don't lose control, as long as you use AI deliberately.

**Current focus: context engineering.** Not writing better prompts, but giving AI the right context, information and structure so it can act as a sparring partner in software design: architecture, domain modelling and recording decisions before any code is written. FlowDock is where I practise this.

**Career direction.** I'm exploring growth toward AI engineering and platform engineering. I'm deliberately not choosing yet. First I'm building the foundation both need (Docker, Linux, HTTP and networking, CI/CD) so I can choose based on experience rather than on what sounds most interesting today.

---

## How I learn

- I ask *why* before *how*. I want to know which problem a technology solves before I use it.
- I learn best from one project that keeps growing, not from separate tutorials. A new technology comes in when the project runs into the problem it solves.
- Starting point for platform topics: no hands-on Docker, CI/CD or Kubernetes experience yet.
